The Olivo Family

Four generations, twenty-six entities, thirty-four accounts, and one frozen-lasagna fortune.

Dominic Olivo started importing olive oil out of a Bridgeport warehouse in 1974, moved into frozen Italian specialties when a supermarket buyer asked for a lasagna that "tastes like somebody's grandmother made it", and sold Olivo Foods to Northline Foods in 2014 for $1.1 billion, mostly in Northline stock. He is 75, remarried, and still drives the Ferrari. The money sits in a Delaware partnership run by his eldest son, a dynasty trust the children cannot touch, three real-estate companies, a Napa vineyard that loses a little money every year on purpose, an art company his daughter curates, and a foundation that gives away $2.3 million a year. His youngest son runs a venture fund in San Francisco and keeps his own affairs separate. His youngest child is thirteen.

Every person, entity, account, holding and loan on this page is invented. Tickers are real listed securities with illustrative prices; CUSIPs are masked. The numbers reconcile: each entity's net worth includes what it owns through other entities, and the family total counts every entity once.

Why this family is a good test
  • A concentrated public position with a loan against it. 2.2 million Northline shares across three vehicles at a $12 basis, $20M borrowed against them. Diversification, gain budgets, gifting and the GRAT all collide here.
  • A blended family. A second spouse with a prenup and a SLAT, a thirteen-year-old half-sister to adults in their forties, and a bypass trust that excludes the second marriage entirely. Visibility rules get exercised.
  • Every custody and authority combination. Managed accounts at Altruist, advised accounts at Goldman and Schwab, read-only IRAs and 529s at Fidelity, assets with no custodian at all.
  • Replication Studio material. A QQQ position to replicate, a direct-indexed S&P sleeve already running, a defined-outcome ETF, two structured notes (one autocallable, one buffered), and an options overlay at Interactive Brokers.
  • Marketplace material. A 2nd Act SPV co-investment, a venture fund where a family member is the GP, angel positions, a sports-team stake, and a private company with a board seat.
  • Operating businesses and real assets. A winery with employees and a bank loan, three industrial buildings on a lease that expires in 2029, a $31M art collection with a credit line against it, ten classic cars, and a jewellery schedule.
  • Charitable plumbing. A private foundation with an excess-business-holdings problem, a DAF, a CRUT paying the founder, and an ILIT paying premiums through Crummey gifts.
