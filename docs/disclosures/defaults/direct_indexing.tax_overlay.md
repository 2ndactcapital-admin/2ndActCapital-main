# Direct indexing and the tax overlay — risks and disclosures

*Default — have counsel review. Written by the platform as a starting point; it is not tax, legal or estate advice.*

## 1. What it is

Direct indexing holds the individual securities of an index (or a model) directly in your account instead of a
fund. Holding the securities lets the adviser sell positions at a loss to harvest the loss for tax purposes, buy a
similar security, and keep the portfolio close to its target.

## 2. Scope and limitations

- **Eligible accounts.** Taxable accounts benefit from harvesting; tax-deferred accounts (IRAs) do not.
- **Minimums.** A minimum account size applies so that the portfolio can hold enough names.
- **Fees.** The firm's fee and trading costs apply in addition to any fees of securities held.
- **Who trades.** The adviser places the trades. The custodian holds the securities and reports cost basis.

## 3. Investment risks

- **Market risk.** The value of your portfolio will rise and fall with the market. You can lose money.
- **Tracking error.** Holdings, exclusions, minimum trade sizes and harvesting make the portfolio's return differ
  from the index's, sometimes by a meaningful amount.

## 4. Tax risks and limits

- **Wash sales.** A loss is disallowed if you buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days
  before or after the sale — **in any of your accounts, including accounts held elsewhere and your spouse's
  accounts.** The platform can only see the accounts it knows about.
- **The benefit varies.** Harvesting opportunities depend on markets. Benefits tend to be **front-loaded**: they are
  usually largest in the first years and shrink as the portfolio's cost basis falls.
- **Deferral, not elimination.** Harvesting lowers your cost basis, so selling later realizes larger gains. The tax
  is usually deferred, not eliminated.
- **Limits on use.** Net capital losses offset capital gains; only **$3,000 a year** (or $1,500 if married filing
  separately) of net losses can offset ordinary income. Unused losses carry forward.
- **Tax law can change**, and the IRS may disagree with how a trade is treated.

## 5. Complexity and cost-basis reporting

The account will hold many positions and generate many trades. Your tax reporting (Form 1099-B) will be longer, and
cost basis for transferred-in securities depends on the custodian's records.

## 6. Leaving the strategy

Liquidating the portfolio, or moving to a fund, can realize large gains and create a tax bill. Transferring the
securities in kind keeps them, but they then need to be managed elsewhere.

## 7. Hypothetical estimates

Any estimate of tax savings or of past performance is hypothetical and subject to the firm's
hypothetical-performance policy and its disclosure.

## 8. Not tax, legal or estate advice

Talk to your tax adviser and attorney about your own situation.
