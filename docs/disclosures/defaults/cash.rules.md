# Cash rules

*Default — have counsel review. Written by the platform as a starting point.*

## 1. What a rule does

A cash rule is a standing instruction the firm sets up with you — for example, keep a target balance in an account,
sweep part of each deposit to savings, or move a fixed amount every month. When a rule runs, it **prepares** a payment
instruction between two of your own accounts and asks a person at the firm to approve it.

## 2. What a rule does not do

A rule never approves, sends or moves money by itself. Every payment it prepares waits for a second person at the firm
to approve it, and is then sent to your custodian the same way as any other payment instruction. A rule never pays
anyone other than the owner of the account it draws from.

## 3. Limits

A rule prepares nothing when the account's balance is out of date, when the account does not hold enough to cover the
payment (it never prepares a partial overdraft), or beyond the per-run and per-day limits set for it. Anything held back
is shown to the firm with the reason.

## 4. Dates

Payments are dated on U.S. business days. A rule that runs on a weekend or holiday either waits for the next business
day or, for a daily rule, does nothing that day.

## 5. Records

Every rule, every change to it, every run and every payment it prepares is recorded. Account numbers are shown by their
last four digits only.
