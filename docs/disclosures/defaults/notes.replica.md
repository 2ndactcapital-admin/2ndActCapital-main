# Structured-note replica versus the note — disclosures

*Default — have counsel review.*

## What the replica is

A structured note is a debt obligation of its issuer: its payoff (buffers, caps, coupons, autocalls) is a promise of
that issuer. A replica builds a similar payoff from listed options and securities held in your account.

## How they differ

- **Credit risk.** The note depends on the issuer being able to pay; if the issuer fails, you can lose some or all
  of your investment. The replica has no issuer credit risk, but nobody promises its payoff either.
- **Payoff.** The replica approximates the note's terms. Its result can differ because of option prices, timing,
  dividends, early assignment and rebalancing.
- **Fees.** The note's fees are embedded in its price. The comparison of the note's estimated value with its price
  to the public is an **estimate** from the issuer's documents.
- **Tax.** A note may be treated as a prepaid forward or a contingent payment debt instrument; options in the
  replica may be Section 1256 contracts and may form straddles. The treatments differ. Ask your tax adviser.
- **Liquidity.** A note may be hard to sell before maturity. The replica's options trade on exchanges, but can be
  thinly traded.

## Estimates and hypothetical results

Any estimate of the note's embedded fees or of hypothetical results is subject to the firm's hypothetical-performance
policy and its disclosure.
