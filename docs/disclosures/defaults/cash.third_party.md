# Third-party payments

*Default — have counsel review. Written by the platform as a starting point. Third-party payments are NOT offered
until counsel has signed off.*

## 1. What a payee is

A payee is a person or organization your household may pay who is not one of your own accounts — a vendor, a charity,
a person, or an outside entity. The firm keeps a register of your payees. Each payee is approved by a second person at
the firm before it is active.

## 2. Bank details

The firm does not store a payee's full account or routing number. It records only the reference your custodian gives
the standing instruction, and the last four digits of the account, so that both you and the firm can recognise it.

## 3. Changes of bank details

A request to change a payee's bank details is the most common route of payment fraud. Every such request suspends the
payee until a second person at the firm has called the payee back on a number already on file — never a number given
in the request — and recorded that call. Until then no payment to that payee can be made.

## 4. Standing Letters of Authorization

A payment to a third party requires a Standing Letter of Authorization (SLOA) you signed with your custodian, naming the
payee and the account it may be paid from. A payee whose SLOA is missing, expired or revoked cannot be paid.

## 5. Custody and finality

Your custodian holds your assets and moves the money; the firm prepares and approves instructions. A payment sent to a
third party may not be recoverable once it has been sent, even if it was sent in error.

## 6. Invoices

An invoice you or the firm upload is recorded as a firm obligation of the household so that your cash coverage shows
it. Recording an invoice does not pay it.
