# Section 351 ETF conversion (exit) — risks and disclosures

*Default — have counsel review. Written by the platform as a starting point; it is not tax or legal advice.*

## 1. What it is

Under Section 351 of the Internal Revenue Code, property contributed to a corporation in exchange for its stock can
be tax-free when the contributors control the corporation right after the exchange. Some newly launched ETFs accept
in-kind contributions of investors' securities on this basis, so that a concentrated or low-basis portfolio can move
into a diversified fund without selling.

## 2. The tests the contribution must meet

- **Diversification (the 25 % / 50 % test).** Each contributor's portfolio must be diversified: no more than **25 %**
  of its value in any one issuer, and no more than **50 %** in **five or fewer** issuers. ETFs and funds you
  contribute are **looked through** to their holdings for this test.
- **Control (the 80 % test).** The contributors, together, must own at least **80 %** of the ETF immediately after
  the exchange. This depends on other contributors and on the launch.

## 3. Eligible owners

- U.S. persons.
- Individual, joint and trust accounts, and S corporations.
- Partnerships and LLCs only after review.
- **Not eligible**: C corporations and non-U.S. persons.

## 4. Ineligible assets

Restricted or illiquid securities, derivatives (including options), cryptocurrency, physical commodities, commodity
pools, grantor trusts and partnership interests are generally not accepted.

## 5. Basis and holding period

Your shares of the ETF generally take the **carryover basis** and **holding period** of the securities contributed.
How each custodian records and reports this differs; check the custodian's statements after the exchange.

## 6. Risks

- **A failed contribution is taxable.** If the tests are not met — or the ETF does not launch as described — the
  exchange can be treated as a taxable sale, and the carryover basis is lost.
- **New-fund risk.** The ETF has no operating history, may not attract enough assets to reach a viable size, and
  could close, which can itself be a taxable event.
- **Complexity.** Eligibility depends on facts that may not be known until the launch.
- **Market risk.** After the exchange, the value of your ETF shares will rise and fall with the market.

## 7. Before you decide

This is not tax or legal advice. Consult your tax adviser, and read the ETF's prospectus before contributing.
