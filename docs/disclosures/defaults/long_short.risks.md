# Long/short extension (130/30, 150/50) — risks and disclosures

*Default — have counsel review. Written by the platform as a starting point; it is not legal or tax advice.*

## What the strategy does

For every $100 of your capital, a 130/30 strategy holds about $130 in stocks it favours and sells about $30 of stocks
it likes least short. A 150/50 strategy holds about $150 long and $50 short. The net exposure stays near $100. The
extra positions are funded by the short sales and require a margin account with a custodian that permits short
selling and stock borrowing.

## Investment and tax risks

1. **Pre-tax underperformance.** The strategy can trail its benchmark before tax. Short positions lose money when
   the stocks the strategy likes least go up.
2. **The tax benefit varies.** Harvestable losses depend on markets. In some years there may be few or none.
3. **Lower future tax rates** would reduce the value of losses carried forward.
4. **Unused losses.** Losses may go unused if there are not enough gains, or ordinary income, to offset them.
5. **A larger tax bill at redemption.** Harvesting lowers your cost basis, so unwinding the strategy can realize
   large gains.
6. **Rule changes or an IRS challenge.** Tax law may change, and the IRS may challenge how a trade is treated
   (wash-sale, constructive-sale and straddle rules among others). Gains and losses on short positions are
   generally short-term.

## Margin and short-sale risks

7. **Unlimited loss on a short.** A stock's price can rise without limit, so a short position can lose more than
   the proceeds of the sale.
8. **Margin calls.** If your account's equity falls below the custodian's maintenance requirement, you may have to
   deposit cash or securities quickly. The custodian may sell positions without notice and at a loss.
9. **Borrow recall.** The lender of borrowed shares can recall them at any time, forcing a short to be closed,
   possibly at a bad price.
10. **Borrow costs change.** The fee to borrow a stock can rise sharply, and any short rebate can fall or disappear.
    Hard-to-borrow stocks can become very expensive to hold short.
